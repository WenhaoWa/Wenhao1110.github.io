## Arctic Underwater Soundscapes & Glacial Noise

Glacial fjords in the Arctic present extremely dynamic and heterogeneous underwater acoustic environments. Intense acoustic transients generated by submarine glacier melting, iceberg calving, and sea ice motion dominate the background noise field. 

Our current work investigates multi-year acoustic mooring and oceanographic observations from **Bowdoin Fjord, Northwestern Greenland**, focusing on:
* Soundscape dynamics driven by ice melting, calving events, and seasonal environmental shifts.
* Numerical propagation modeling (using BELLHOP and RAM) to evaluate acoustic transmission loss within shallow, heterogeneous water layers under varying seasonal sea ice cover.

## Narwhal Vocalization & Biosonar Tracking

Marine mammals such as narwhals (*Monodon monoceros*) rely on high-frequency biosonar for navigation and foraging in ice-covered Arctic waters. However, acoustic analysis in glacial fjords faces severe challenges due to low signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) caused by cryospheric ambient noise.

Our methodological developments include:
* **Transient Detection:** Developing robust detection pipelines based on Teager-Kaiser Energy Operators (TKEO) and automated pulse train association.
* **Self-Supervised Feature Learning:** Utilizing AudioMAE and Transformer architectures to extract high-dimensional acoustic representations without relying heavily on sparse manual annotations.
* **Behavioral Analysis:** Quantifying echolocation click train patterns, inter-click interval (ICI) distributions, and buzz sequences relative to glacier calving events.

## Fisheries Acoustics & Physics-Informed Methods

Beyond Arctic bioacoustics, we explore broader biological acoustic inversion and target classification methods:
* Broadband acoustic target strength (TS) modeling of marine organisms.
* Physics-informed machine learning frameworks combining echo statistics with scattering models.
* Passive and active acoustic observation synergies for marine ecosystem monitoring.
